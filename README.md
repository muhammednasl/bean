# Premium Restaurant Website — Responsive UI Design

Design a premium, modern, visually appealing restaurant website with an elegant black-and-cream color palette. The website should feel sophisticated, welcoming, minimal, and photography-focused, with excellent responsive behavior across mobile, tablet, and desktop.

The goal is to create a distinctive restaurant website with a strong visual identity, beautiful food photography, clean typography, and intuitive navigation. Avoid generic restaurant templates.

## 1. Design System

**Color palette**
- Main outer page background: warm cream (#F4EBDD).
- Hero section background: deep black (#111111).
- Primary text on black: warm cream (#F4EBDD).
- Text on cream: near-black (#171717).
- Accent color: muted antique gold (#B89A64), used sparingly.
- Buttons: cream background with black text, with subtle hover transitions.

**Typography**
- Use an elegant editorial serif font for major headings.
- Use a clean, modern sans-serif for body text and navigation.
- Make headings distinctive, readable, and well-spaced.
- Use a premium editorial style rather than oversized text everywhere.

**Overall layout**
- The entire website sits on a warm cream background.
- Use a centered outer container with comfortable margins and consistent spacing.
- Place the black hero section inside the cream outer layout.
- Give the hero section a small, elegant border radius.
- Keep the website spacious, minimal, and content-focused.
- Avoid excessive cards, decorative icons, gradients, and unnecessary animations.

## 2. Navigation

Create a responsive navbar with these links:

- Home
- About Us
- Menu
- Our Location
- Gallery
- Contact Us

**Desktop navigation**
- Restaurant logo on the left.
- Navigation links arranged horizontally.
- A clean, elegant appearance with balanced spacing.
- Use subtle hover effects.

**Mobile navigation**
- Display the restaurant logo on the left.
- Display a hamburger menu button on the right.
- Keep the Home navigation item visible only inside the mobile menu, not as a separate desktop navigation link.
- When the hamburger menu is clicked, show a full-screen or spacious overlay menu with all six navigation links.
- Include a close button and make the menu easy to use on mobile.
- Close the menu when a navigation link is selected.

Make the logo, menu button, and navigation links properly aligned at all screen sizes.

## 3. Hero Section — Most Important Section

Create a beautiful, responsive, two-column hero section inside the rounded black container.

**Content**
- Small eyebrow text: “A TASTE WORTH REMEMBERING”
- Main heading: “Where Every Flavour Tells a Story.”
- Short supporting paragraph: “Discover thoughtfully crafted dishes, exceptional ingredients, and a dining experience made to be remembered.”
- Primary button: “Explore Our Menu”
- Secondary text link: “Discover Our Story”

Use these as placeholder copy that can be replaced with the restaurant's real brand content.

**Desktop layout**
- Left side: heading, supporting paragraph, and buttons.
- Right side: one large, high-quality food photograph.
- Vertically align the content and image in a balanced composition.
- Let the photo occupy a generous portion of the hero width.
- Keep the image visually dominant without overpowering the text.
- Maintain comfortable spacing between the text and photo.

**Mobile layout**
- The logo and hamburger navigation appear at the top.
- Place the hero text below the navigation.
- Place the food photograph below the hero text, aligned to the right.
- Use a balanced, editorial composition with the image right-aligned.
- Ensure the text is readable and the image is not squeezed into a tiny thumbnail.
- The black hero background must cover the entire hero content area.
- All hero text must use warm cream colors.

**Responsive transformation**
- As the viewport width increases, progressively increase the food photograph's displayed size.
- At larger tablet and desktop breakpoints, transition to a side-by-side layout.
- At desktop widths, the text must be on the left and the large photograph on the right.
- The photo should grow naturally with the available layout width, using responsive sizing rather than a fixed small width.
- Preserve the hero's subtle border radius and the surrounding cream outer layout.
- Prevent overflow, awkward wrapping, and excessive empty space.

The hero must look intentionally designed at every breakpoint, not simply like a mobile layout stretched across a desktop screen.

## 4. Short About Section on the Homepage

Directly below the hero, add a small introduction to the restaurant on the cream background.

**Heading:** “Our Story, Served with Passion.”

Include only two or three concise sentences about the restaurant's culinary philosophy, ingredients, hospitality, or heritage.

Add a small “Read Our Story” link that navigates to the full About Us section or page.

Important: Do not include the full restaurant history on the homepage. Keep this section short, elegant, and easy to scan. Visitors can use the About Us navigation link to explore the complete story.

## 5. Menu Preview Section

Create a compact preview of the restaurant menu.

- Heading: “A Few of Our Favourites”
- Display a small selection of signature dishes using beautiful food photographs.
- Include dish names and short descriptions.
- Show prices only when actual prices are supplied.
- Include a “View Full Menu” button that navigates to the complete menu section or page.
- Use a refined editorial grid with minimal visual decoration.
- Maintain the black-and-cream visual identity throughout.

Keep the homepage menu preview concise. The complete menu should be accessible through the Menu navigation link.

## 6. Testimonials Section

Place a testimonial section after the menu preview.

**Heading:** “Words from Our Guests”

- Display two or three short customer testimonials.
- Use a clean layout with elegant typography.
- Keep the testimonial cards or columns visually minimal.
- Include customer names and ratings only when verified information is provided.
- Use subtle separators or understated accents instead of heavy borders.
- Make the section feel authentic, warm, and trustworthy.

Do not add a Gallery section or Contact section to the homepage. These must be accessible through their respective navigation links.

## 7. Footer

Create a sophisticated footer with a black background and cream-colored typography.

Organize the footer into four clear groups.

**Column 1 — Brand**
- Restaurant logo.
- A short, one-sentence brand description.

**Column 2 — Quick Links**
- About Us
- Menu
- Our Location
- Gallery
- Contact Us

**Column 3 — Contact**
- Email address
- Phone number

Use clearly identifiable placeholders if real contact details have not been provided. Do not invent real email addresses or phone numbers.

**Column 4 — Follow Us**
- Instagram link and icon.
- Facebook link and icon.

Use actual clickable social links when provided. Keep the icons simple and consistent.

Add a subtle divider and a copyright line at the bottom of the footer.

## 8. Page and Navigation Behavior

Create the following navigation destinations:

- Home: the homepage hero.
- About Us: the full restaurant story.
- Menu: the complete restaurant menu.
- Our Location: address, opening hours, and directions.
- Gallery: a dedicated food and restaurant photography gallery.
- Contact Us: a dedicated contact section or page with the restaurant's real contact information.

These destinations can be separate pages or well-structured sections, depending on the website architecture.

Keep the homepage focused on the hero, short About introduction, menu preview, and testimonials. Do not duplicate the complete About Us, Gallery, or Contact content on the homepage.

Make all buttons and links functional. Use smooth scrolling for same-page navigation where appropriate.

## 9. Image Direction

Use high-quality, realistic restaurant photography.

- Select one outstanding food photograph for the hero.
- Prefer warm, natural lighting and professional food styling.
- Use imagery that complements the cream-and-black design.
- Maintain consistent image quality and composition throughout the menu preview.
- Use proper image cropping and responsive image sizing.
- Avoid random stock photos that do not match the restaurant's cuisine.
- Preserve image proportions and use carefully chosen object positioning.

Use supplied restaurant assets whenever available. Do not invent restaurant-specific branding, dishes, or factual details.

## 10. Responsive and Accessibility Requirements

Design for:
- Mobile: 320–767px.
- Tablet: 768–1023px.
- Desktop: 1024px and above.

Requirements:
- Mobile-first responsive layout.
- No horizontal scrolling.
- Correct text wrapping at every breakpoint.
- Responsive food images that grow appropriately with the available width.
- Consistent outer margins and spacing.
- Accessible text contrast.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Descriptive image alt text.
- Touch-friendly mobile navigation and buttons.

## 11. Final Design Quality

The final website should feel like a premium independent restaurant brand with strong editorial art direction.

Prioritize:
1. The black-and-cream rounded hero layout.
2. The precise mobile-to-desktop image and text transformation.
3. Elegant typography and generous whitespace.
4. Beautiful food photography.
5. Simple, functional navigation.
6. A short, uncluttered homepage.
7. A sophisticated footer with quick links, contact details, and social links.

Create a complete, high-fidelity responsive design. Do not stop at a wireframe. Keep all sections visually consistent, polished, and ready for implementation.
